# ADR 0006: Publish negative results
Status: Accepted · Date: 2026-09-17 (decision made earlier, recorded now)

## Context
A revenue system has many ways to look like it is working while it is not. METHOD.md opens by saying that each section states the mechanism and then the failure it exists to prevent, and that the failures are the interesting part; README.md adds that several of those failures are ones this project shipped and then had to find. A document that recorded only the mechanisms that paid off would be shorter, more flattering and less useful, because a reader could not tell which claims had survived measurement and which had never been tested.

## Decision
Negative and null results are published in the main documents with the same weight as positive ones, and a prediction that turned out wrong is kept on the record rather than edited away. This is a standing policy of the project, not an accident of one section. The concrete instance: section 13 predicted that summing nightly bid prices was the place a network formulation would earn its keep. When section 14 built the model and found that length of stay alone earns nothing, the original words were quoted in section 14 and section 13 was given a pointer saying one of its sentences was wrong, instead of the sentence being silently rewritten. The same policy governs the rate experiment, whose honest summary is that it is methodologically correct and buys no measurable revenue, and the first version of that experiment, which made the estimate worse and is described in full. Numbers that have not been measured are said to be unmeasured rather than estimated, and README.md carries a section titled Three things I measured and did not like.

## Consequences
The document is longer and some of its most prominent numbers are unflattering: the engine given the true price response earning 0.38% less than the biased one, a first experiment at +123% error, a network model that loses a percent or more on hotels without the right shape, a length of stay effect of seven wins in twelve. In exchange, every positive claim sits next to the test that could have falsified it, tests are named after the conclusions they guard, and the +6.4% RevPAR lift over the incumbent is presented as believable precisely because a forty percent lift would have meant the baseline was rigged. Every record in this folder inherits the rule: a Proposed mechanism becomes Accepted or Rejected on numbers, and a Rejected record stays in the folder.

## Evidence
METHOD.md, opening paragraph: the failures are the interesting part. METHOD.md section 13, final paragraph, and section 14, opening: the original prediction that nightly summing was "exactly where a network formulation would earn its keep", now quoted and marked as wrong. METHOD.md section 14, Which dimension it actually needed: one room type with real stays wins seven trials out of twelve. METHOD.md section 14, What this changes about the engine: the in-backtest gain is stated as not measured. METHOD.md section 12: the 0.38% null result, the +123% first version, and the honest summary of the feature. METHOD.md section 5, What is not claimed: the 27% and 12% bias stated rather than hidden. README.md, Three things I measured and did not like, and Honesty about what this is: a six percent lift is believable, forty percent would mean the baseline was rigged.
