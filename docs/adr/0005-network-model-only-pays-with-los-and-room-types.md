# ADR 0005: The network model pays only with both length of stay and room types
Status: Accepted · Date: 2026-09-17 (decision made earlier, recorded now)

## Context
Sections 1 to 12 of METHOD.md price one room type with a single resource bid price, the closed form of section 7, and handle multi night stays by summing nightly bid prices. A real hotel has five to fifteen room types with guests substituting between them, which is a network problem, and section 13 predicted that the summing approximation was where a network formulation would earn its keep. The open question was whether the machinery is worth its weight, and for which hotels.

## Decision
pace/network.py builds the network model as a separate layer with its own command, python3 run.py network. Products are stays, resources are (room type, night) cells, and the dual prices of the deterministic linear program are the cell bid prices, solved by exact coordinate descent on the dual with a feasible primal plan and a reported duality gap. A prorated stochastic variant puts demand uncertainty back by spreading each stay's value across its nights in proportion to the duals and solving each night with the section 7 closed form; proration was chosen over displacement adjustment so the nightly parts still sum to a statement about the stay rather than its length. Guests substitute through a nested logit in pace/choice.py that reduces exactly to the section 5 acceptance curve with one room type, or with several types priced in step. Room types are opt in through pace/roomtypes.py. PaceEngine is not changed: it still runs the section 7 closed form, and the network layer is for properties that genuinely have both multi night demand and several room types.

## Consequences
Against the nightly bid price, paired over twelve trials on a twenty one night window, the deterministic dual earns +1.47% at demand 0.75, +1.81% at 0.90 (96.1% occupancy, twelve wins out of twelve), +0.61% at 1.00 and +0.34% at 1.20, decaying as the house fills. Switching each dimension off at demand 0.90 shows where the gain lives. Four room types with real stays: +1.81%. Four types with all one night stays: -1.06%, zero wins in twelve. One type with real stays: +0.10%, seven wins in twelve, a coin. One type with one night stays: -0.84%. Neither dimension alone earns anything; the whole gain is the interaction, because a group block needs the same room type on every night of its stay, which is one more binding constraint than an additive nightly price can express. Where the problem is not a network, the machinery loses a percent or more against the arithmetic it replaces, so a property with one room type or no multi night demand should decline it. One more result is recorded against the obvious ordering: the deterministic dual beats the prorated stochastic form, +1.81% against +1.01%, despite discarding demand uncertainty. It is not better calibrated. Proration understates what a booking costs when it needs several cells at once and the deterministic dual overstates it, two errors of opposite sign that happen to cancel, so neither form is taken as right. The gain has not been measured inside the full backtest with the engine's own forecast, and that number is absent from the documentation because it does not yet exist.
