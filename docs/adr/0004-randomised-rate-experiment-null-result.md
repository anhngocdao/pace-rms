# ADR 0004: Randomised rate experiment, and its null result
Status: Accepted · Date: 2026-09-17 (decision made earlier, recorded now)

## Context
The price response in pace/elasticity.py is fitted on the hotel's own history, and that history is biased: every rate in it was set by looking at how full the night already was, so rate and demand were decided together. The observational fit returns an elasticity of 2.65 for retail against a true 2.08 and 2.82 for the online channel against a true 2.52, over elastic by 27% and 12%. Class fixed effects and shrinkage limit the damage but do not remove it. Removing it needs variation that somebody deliberately created.

## Decision
pace/experiment.py assigns each stay date, once and at random, a multiplier on whatever rate the optimizer would otherwise publish: ten control slots against eight treated at plus or minus eight and sixteen percent, with the arm chosen by a blake2b hash of the date so it can be recomputed for any past night without being stored. The perturbation is skipped when the night is forecast to sell out and clipped when it would push the rate below the bid price floor. Because compliance is therefore partial, the estimator treats the assigned arm as an instrument for the rate actually charged, two stage least squares with a saturated first stage. The engine measures its own first stage and refuses to use an estimate when the arms have not separated, with MIN_INSTRUMENT_SPREAD = 0.045. The feature stays in the repository, run by python3 run.py experiment, and is documented as measurement. It is not credited with revenue.

## Consequences
The experiment does what it claims. Retail identification error falls from +27% to +10%; the online channel barely moves, +12% to +11%, because it books further out and a larger share of its volume lands outside the perturbed window. It costs 0.91% of RevPAR over the scored window, the price of publishing a rate nobody chose on roughly forty percent of nights. Then the null result: a third engine handed the true price response directly, which only a synthetic market allows, earned 0.38% less RevPAR than the biased engine. Revenue is flat at its own maximum. Believing 2.65 when the truth is 2.08 moves the chosen rate by four dollars and costs between 0.05% and 0.11% of contribution; on a single floating segment every rate inside a sixteen dollar band returns 99% of the best available, and with the real mix, where nearly forty percent of business is contracted, the band widens to twenty four dollars. What the experiment buys is the knowledge that the error is small, which cannot be had without measuring the curve, and a statement of where the money is not: the six percent over the incumbent comes from the forecast, the unconstraining and the controls. A test is named after this conclusion. The first version is recorded as well, because it made retail worse, +123% error, when only twelve percent of the assigned variation survived into the rate actually charged.
