# ADR 0003: Unconstrain censored demand by projection detruncation
Status: Accepted · Date: 2026-09-17 (decision made earlier, recorded now)

## Context
By ADR 0002 the engine sees only the ledger, so a night that sold out is right censored: true demand was somewhere above the rooms booked and nobody wrote the number down. Nights were also priced differently from one another, so their booked counts are not directly comparable. A forecast trained on the raw ledger concludes that a night which sold 150 rooms had demand of 150, and opens the same night at the same rate next year. METHOD.md calls this the most common way a revenue system quietly teaches itself to under-price, and it bites hardest on the compressed nights where the rate decision is worth the most.

## Decision
pace/unconstrain.py applies two corrections, in order. First price: each night's rooms per segment are divided by the acceptance rate at the rate that night actually charged, so dates priced differently are restated at a common reference; contracted segments divide by one. Then censoring, by projection detruncation following Weatherford and Bodily. Demand within a demand class, a season band paired with a day of week, is assumed normal; each censored observation is replaced by its conditional expectation above the censoring point, E[X | X > c] = mu + sigma * phi(z) / (1 - Phi(z)) with z = (c - mu) / sigma; the class is re-estimated; and the loop repeats until it settles. Observable denials from the ledger are added back before the detruncation runs, because they are demand the hotel did see. The elasticity fit in pace/elasticity.py drops sold out nights for the same reason.

## Consequences
The forecast is built on an estimate of demand rather than of sales. House wide the correction is around eight percent; on the film festival nights it is far larger, and those are the only nights where the rate decision is worth much. The method rests on the normality assumption within a class and on a class having enough observations to estimate, which is why the ledger contract in ADR 0002 asks for denials: a property management system that does not log them still runs the engine, but learns less about exactly the nights that matter most. The unconstrainer is a replaceable module; anything that keeps ClassDemand.mean, .booked_mean and .censoring_uplift can be dropped in without the control layer noticing.
