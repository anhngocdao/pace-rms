# ADR 0002: The market emits requests, not bookings
Status: Accepted · Date: 2026-09-17 (decision made earlier, recorded now)

## Context
The engine is scored by replaying one simulated market under three policies: a static rate, the seasonal ladder of a well run incumbent, and Pace itself. If the simulator produced bookings, a booking refused by one policy would simply vanish, and a comparison between policies would be a re-scoring of the same sales rather than a counterfactual. The other half of the problem is what the engine is permitted to know. A real property management system records what it sold and the refusals it could see. It has no record of the guest who saw the rate and left, and a system trained on the request stream would be learning from data no hotel has.

## Decision
The simulator in pace/simulate.py generates requests. Demand per arrival date and segment is a Poisson count, and each request draws its own lead time, length of stay and willingness to pay, the last from a lognormal centred on the rate its segment is normally quoted, with cancellation and no-show drawn at booking time. A request refused by one policy is still available to another on the same replay. Four segments are modelled and only two float with the public rate; negotiated corporate and group pay a contracted rate that does not move when the BAR moves. The engine never reads the request stream. It reads pace/ledger.py, which holds accepted bookings, the on-the-books picture at every past lead time, and only the denials a hotel could actually log: no room, below the minimum stay, closed to arrival, rate closed. Price refusals are not in the ledger.

## Consequences
Replaying the same stream under different policies is a true counterfactual, so the +6.4% RevPAR lift over the incumbent on 184 settled nights is a like for like number, and it holds at +6.4% to +7.7% across four independently drawn markets. Demand on sold out nights reaches the engine censored, exactly as in production, which is what makes the unconstraining in ADR 0003 necessary rather than decorative. The engine also carries a blind spot on purpose: in the backtest window it lost 6,700 room nights on price and never knew. Getting the segment contract right is part of the same decision. An earlier version let all four segments float, corporate fitted as almost perfectly inelastic, and the optimizer walked every peak night to the rate ceiling; the bug was in the model of the world, not in the optimizer.
